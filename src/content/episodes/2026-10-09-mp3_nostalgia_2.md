At Mailtape, we sometimes assume we're a bit old-fashioned, from the generation of Walkmans, those from the 90s, into which we'd put old cassettes, sometimes recorded ourselves. Then came portable CD players (with scratched CDs and those that skipped some parts), and then the first MP3 players. Since then, it's been a musical orgy. But some of us have kept the spirit of the MP3 player: carefully collecting fabulous tracks, downloading them, and listening to them on our little device, in various contexts, often intimate.

Some of these tracks have stood the test of time. They're still there, stuck in the player, unable to escape, because they're too tasty, too precious, too essential... You can't imagine the joy we feel in bringing them out of obscurity and sharing some of them with you! This is MP3 Nostalgia!

For this episode, Ling, one of our curators, digs deep into his memories. 

Growing up in Shanghai, China, Ling received his first SONY MP3 player from his parents on his thirteenth birthday. In the early 2000s, mainstream Chinese music media offered limited exposure to niche sounds, while access to music downloads remained scarce. Yet, somehow, the handful of tracks he managed to load onto that small device would go on to leave a lasting impression, quietly shaping the musical sensibilities that define him as a DJ today.

# Curator's selection

## John Adams - The Chairman Dances (Foxtrot for Orchestra)

 Ling: **"** My earliest introduction to the general idea of *western music* - not the music genre from western movies, but what Chinese called any music from western culture in the Asian point of view, was through movie soundtracks. I was a movie fan first before I got into music. For this one, it opened the Luca Guadagnino's *Io sono l'amore*. I was simply listening to it during the movie and fell in love with its playful yet richly layered orchestral minimalism, without even knowing who John Adams is, or his approaches as a composer, which I became aware of much later on.**"**

## Luis Bacalov - Il Grande Duello  (Parte Prima)

 Ling: **"** Taken from Quentin Tarantino’s *Kill Bill: Vol. 1*, another film I adored as a teenager, this track accompanies one of my favourite scenes: the animated chapter, *The Origin of O-Ren*.  The eclecticism of many soundtracks that I used to listen to growing up has a big influence of storytelling on my track selections of my DJ sets**"** 

## The Sore Feet Song - Ally Kerr

 Ling: **"** I watched a lot of animés, so naturally I was equally drawn to their soundtracks. This one is the open title of *Mushishi*, a niche animé that blurs boundaries between folklore, nature, and human existance. I remember that I was listening to it in my earphones often when I would walk to school in early mornings... **"**

## 王菲 (Faye Wong) - Eyes On Me

 Ling: **"** This one had stayed on my MP3 probably for the longest time - not only because it's from Faye Wong, one of her very few songs recorded in English language, but also because it left an impression on me when I was only eight, from a DVD which complied some music videos. They included this title but with some 3D animation. I had no idea that the song and the video came from the PS1 game *Final Fantasy VIII.*  Few years later I came across a cover of the song by Japanese singer Angela Aki, which led me to trace it back to its origin. It's a full circle moment.**"** 

## 王菲 (Faye Wong) - 香奈兒

 Ling: **"** I have to include a second Faye Wong here - she is such an icon in China, maybe even in South Asia. This one is taken from her album *寓言(Fable)*, which took a left turn from her dream-pop signatures where she explores more pop/electronic textures. The song has a clear drum & bass influence on the arrangements, which again I had no knowledge about whatsoever, but yet has made a full circle on my music taste these days. **"**
