 # Guest's selection 
 ## Chaz Knapp - wm jan23 24 (i) 
Weirs: **"** A contemporary practitioner of the minimal, place-based recording practice that _Diamond Grove_ was striving for. Chaz recorded these brief missives to tape outside in the dead of winter, allowing the distortion of environmental sounds and warping strings to become part of the product. This particular piece is a mantra-like guitar-and-vocals tune reminiscent of Mount Eerie's _Dawn_ (another winter record) or the early work of caroline (especially the incredible B-side 'BRJ') but overlaid with the rush of cold water and distorted by the guitar detuning from the cold. If you like this one, check out the Microfolk series Chaz did with Courtney who plays on the Weirs record. **"** 
 ## Dariush Dolat-Shahi - Razm 
Weirs: **"** One of my favorite records from Smithsonian Folkways. Computer oscillators, sample-based loops, and traditional Iranian stringed instruments intertwine to create a haunting yet also undeniably grooving album.This record along with american sources like Flynt and Conrad are foundational texts in Weirs World; both in the way they weave traditional forms with ideas from "art music," and how they use iteration on folk melodies as a deconstructive/transcendent tool. **"** 
 ## Green Plum Ensemble - The Heavenly Music - Cool of the Day 
Weirs: **"** Spare, a cappella singing reminiscent of their source Jean Ritchie or contemporary interpreters like House and Land is interrupted by blasts of discordant woodwind and brass. This is what I imagine the depiction of Death from the Seventh Seal has on his headphones while walking around. I believe the Philadelphia group Green Plum Ensemble is no longer performing but the members are worth following. Everything Sweet Wreath puts out is worth listening to, Weirs members are especially fond of Silica Gel and Vernal Scuzz. **"**
